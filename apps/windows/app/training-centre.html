02 · BEFORE YOU START

Set up an exercise

Tracks and aircraft

Runway orientation and inbound/outbound tracks describe the exercise geometry and review references. They are not automatic join clearances. A runway designator such as 23 is a separate airfield value.

Choose an aircraft profile, then check ground speed in knots, distance in nautical miles and turn rate in degrees per second. Speed and turn rate determine the radius of each curved turn.

Level and airfield

Check initial altitude, aerodrome elevation, QNH, QFE and training vertical rate. Altitude uses QNH, height uses the aerodrome/QFE reference, and flight level uses standard pressure.

The pressure conversion is a training approximation of 27 ft/hPa. Changing a pressure setting changes its reference; it does not move the aircraft instantly.

Callsigns

Use readable text callsigns or three-digit numbers from 100 to 999. In Single QGH you may speak the callsign or omit it. In Tactical, address the intended aircraft for each aircraft instruction.

A new random exercise will not reproduce the previous start. The aircraft may already be turning.

Preflight audio

Connect headphones before enabling audible pilot replies. Play the test, confirm you hear it through your headphones and choose 100, 130 or 170 WPM. This is your confirmation of the output route.

Keep replies muted when using speakers. Text acknowledgements and simulated D/F transmissions remain available.

03 · AT THE CONSOLE

Exercise controls

Normal QGH

Give a left or right turn to a particular heading. The selected turn rate applies until the target is reached. A new direction and target replace the prior instruction.

Report Heading returns the current heading. Report Heading Passing arms a future report and does not assign a turn.

U/S Compass

Use Turn Left Now, Turn Right Now and Stop Turn Now. Left and right are independent: reverse the direction directly when needed.

No actual aircraft heading is provided to the controller. Stop Turn stops the turn while flight continues. Time the manoeuvre with the clock.

D/F display and transmission timing

QDM selects magnetic homing; QTE selects true bearing. During a simulated aircraft transmission, the indication follows that aircraft. On release, the last indication holds for two seconds and then disappears. Muted audio still produces the simulated transmission. Controller ground indication is off.

Close to overhead, bearings can change rapidly or become unusable. Use repeated independent transmissions for confirmation; a single unstable reading is not a reliable position fix.

Clock, speed and one-minute advance

The controller clock has start, stop and reset controls. Speed changes affect the aircraft’s subsequent motion. Advance Flight One Minute moves simulated time forward by sixty seconds while retaining the flight model, turns and report events. Use it deliberately: it can pass several events quickly.

Orbit, Continue Orbit and Resume Normal

Orbit Left or Right starts a continuous curved orbit. Continue Orbit keeps it going. Resume Normal requests the orbit exit back onto the heading held before entry; it is not an immediate jump onto a course. Watch the acknowledgement and review the completed geometry.

Report Heading Passing

This is a report condition in Normal QGH. The selected heading need not be the final target of the turn. A report is triggered when the aircraft actually crosses it. An aircraft that never crosses it has nothing to report. U/S Compass does not disclose actual heading.

Vertical controls and visual reports

Choose the target and reference, then issue Climb or Descend. Maintain Level stops vertical motion at the present altitude. Report Level is immediate; Report Passing and Report Reaching arm later events. Arming a report does not itself clear a climb or descent.

Runway or aerodrome visual reports use the scenario’s range, height and approach gates, together with applicable visibility and overrides. A forced Not Visual state persists until cleared. A visual report does not create a landing clearance.

04 · RADIO WORK · PRESS TO TALK BY DEFAULT

Voice & pilot replies

There is no cloud speech fallback. In Tactical, for example: “Raven Twenty One turn right heading zero six zero”. A unique designator may identify one aircraft; use its full callsign whenever two aircraft share that designator. Confirm termination in the dialog before ending an exercise.

PTT

Hold PTT, speak one clear transmission, then release. Allow the final words to be recognised. Execution follows the completed accepted transmission, before the pilot reply.

The red acknowledgement shows the accepted result; the affected control highlights. Check those results whenever you issue a command.

Continuous Listening

Speak a complete call and leave a short natural pause to finish the transmission. Background speech can be heard by the microphone, so use a quiet environment and headphones for pilot sound.

Turn this mode off when you are not controlling the exercise. Manual controls stay available throughout.

Interrupting a reply

Controller PTT takes priority over pilot audio. A new accepted instruction executes and receives its own acknowledgement. The interrupted audible reply is not played again; the command history retains what happened.

Muting pilot sound does not stop the aircraft, accepted calls, captions or D/F timing.

Reliable recognition

Use the catalogue’s complete phrases, a clear direction and complete digits. Do not rely on one matching word to identify a command. An unclear, conditional or negated call must not produce an unintended manoeuvre.

Use the on-screen control if recognition is unavailable. Once resources are prepared on the device, speech processing and pilot resources are local.

Information received, a report armed and a manoeuvre executed are different outcomes. The acknowledgement tells you which occurred. Unsupported procedures are identified without pretending they were flown.

06 · TWO TO FOUR AIRCRAFT

Tactical & formation

Check every callsign, colour and level before starting. Colour helps tracking, but the callsign is the identity. Address each aircraft explicitly; the radio target highlights for the transmission while your manual selection remains available.

Independent aircraft

Use the callsign tabs to select manual controls and the relevant transmission request. A voice instruction targets its spoken callsign. Do not infer that an instruction went to the aircraft whose tab you last selected.

Initial levels should be separated by 1,000 ft. A new vertical clearance can create a conflict; review the displayed conflict before confirming it.

Formation and detachment

Select the formation leader in setup. Attached followers continue following its geometry; vertical movement preserves their formation offsets. Addressing the leader does not silently assign every follower the same level clearance.

Stop Following Leader detaches an individual follower. It keeps its current flight state until you issue a new instruction. You can then space individual base turns and approaches.

On a phone, the compact aircraft tabs preserve readable callsigns. Check the addressed name in the acknowledgement before the next call.

07 · AFTER TERMINATION

Review & debrief

Terminate the exercise to reveal the flight track. Use replay speed to shorten the debrief, pause at a command, and zoom or fit the track for context. Reference radials and outbound/final approach lines show how the track relates to the configured procedure.

  1. Match command to response.Check the addressed aircraft, accepted instruction and timing. Distinguish flight actions from received information and pending reports.
  2. Read the curved path.Look at the radius of turns, overhead manoeuvres, base-turn direction and the effect of speed. Aircraft cannot make a single-point turn.
  3. Review event timing.Compare report requests with actual crossings, levels and simulated transmissions. A report request is not evidence the event already occurred.
  4. Practise one improvement.Restart a random exercise with the same setup or return to setup to change its demands.

09 · GET BACK TO PRACTICE

Troubleshooting

Voice is unavailable or a call is missed

Check microphone permission for this site and the selected input device. Complete any initial offline voice preparation while connected. Confirm the page is listening, then use a complete catalogue phrase. On Tactical, include the correct callsign. Use manual controls while resolving audio input.

Pilot sound is heard as a command

Mute pilot replies immediately. Connect and test headphones, reduce leakage and confirm the microphone is the intended device. Do not enable audible replies on speakers. Disconnecting headphones can change the audio route even when the browser cannot report the change.

The phone view is too large or obscured

Use the Controls toggle to reveal or hide exercise controls. Move or reset the voice control position. Close any open settings panel. If a stale layout remains after a release, complete the app’s offered update and reopen the page; record the displayed version when reporting the issue.

The installed web app shows an older release

Open it while connected so it can check for an update. Finish the exercise before accepting a reload. Verify the release shown in this guide. Repeated hard refreshes should not be part of normal operation; report persistent version differences with the site address and device.

A video will not save or play offline

Check available storage, allow the complete download and keep the page open until saving finishes. Remove an unneeded saved clip to free space. Clips are saved for this site and release; after an upgrade, use Remove Previous-Version Videos to clear older clips when that button is shown. It does not remove current clips or exercise data.

Private browsing or device cleanup may remove caches. The app reports failures without marking an incomplete clip as available offline. Downloading stops if a file exceeds its declared or permitted size.

A command was acknowledged but no turn began

Check whether it was information, Standby, an armed report, an unsupported instruction or a rejected clearance. Confirm Normal versus U/S Compass and the addressed callsign. In U/S, use the immediate left/right/stop controls. Do not infer execution solely from hearing a pilot voice.
