01 / START HERE

Your first exercise

  1. Open the simulator and choose Open instructor setup. This procedural workspace needs no access-code file. Edit the visible aircraft roster, starting with 101. Aerodrome, Approach and Area procedures share the same exercise; there is no required family selector.
  2. Set the title, runway heading and QNH. Use Configure airspace to prepare the chart, then return to your retained roster. Choose Create session; the exercise opens paused. Creating a session replaces the current attempt, so download Scenario + map first if needed.
  3. Open Session and share its six-digit PIN. Choose Open student in new tab. Move the tab to an extended second monitor, if available, and use its full-screen button. The student enters their name and that PIN and chooses Request to join. The PIN is a join code, separate from the aircraft count of 1–24.
  4. Instructor: choose Admit beside the student's request. Student: choose Ready — open my scope. The instructor can then start the exercise with Run.
  5. Select 101 and use D/F transmission only. Its neutral pilot transmission activates both homing instruments and the student bearing ray without giving a heading or position report. After release, a new setup retains the frozen indication for 10 seconds, then blanks. The instructor can adjust this under Aircraft controls → Bearing display.
  6. Aircraft follow their headings or assigned routes while the clock runs. The student records estimates and sends typed controller calls; the instructor applies the matching structured clearances. Use Pause and Run to pause/resume, and End exercise to stop traffic for review. Choose Reopen exercise to retain the same traffic and records, paused, then Run to continue.

Use Separation to record the chosen method, rule reference, prerequisites and evidence. Compare the student's reports and decisions with instructor truth. This is synthetic training; no microphone or voice control is used.

02 / INSTRUCTOR

A radar-style working picture

The scope fills the working desk. Aircraft symbols, callsigns, altitude, speed and heading describe the instructor's continuous truth picture, independently of what the student has received. While running, the header and help captions are hidden so the operational desk has the space; pausing restores them.

  • Manual controls: directed left/right turns, Stop turn, continuous left/right orbits, Leave orbit / hold, D/F transmission, heading/position reports and release transmission sit along the scope edge on larger displays. Heading, speed and level controls sit below the plot. On compact or short screens, these controls move into the scrollable Aircraft controls drawer. Route, holding and ground actions are also available there.
  • Select: click an aircraft symbol or its traffic card to open its controls.
  • Pan and zoom: drag or scroll on either scope to pan. Ctrl + scroll zooms around the pointer; +/− and the range menu also zoom. Use Pan arrows, or focus the scope and use the keyboard arrows. Centre station or Home returns to the reference station.
  • Declutter: the instructor searches by route name or reporting fix, then ticks individual routes or uses Show/Hide all, Show/Hide matches, or Only matches. Expand Airspace boundaries to filter prohibited, restricted, danger, LFA and CTR areas. Selections update both consoles and survive reconnect/restart. Students can inspect and search the selection but cannot change it. Hiding a route does not close it or cancel aircraft instructions; Chart briefing retains the complete chart.
  • Scope detail & tools: inside Declutter, Routes & fixes and Airspace boundaries switch whole shared layers. Labels, rings, aircraft trails/vectors, runway/final, approach marks and the ruler are local display choices. Reset local display keeps the instructor's chart selection. Raster maps, boundary names and approach marks start off; route names are spaced and fix names appear at local zoom.
  • Route overview / Local view: fit the visible loaded route network, or return to the aerodrome range and reference station. These buttons change only your view.
  • Homing: its dock stays outside the airspace plot. Use D/F ⇄ to move it between sides.
  • Ruler: either desk can enable Declutter → Scope detail & tools → Bearing / range ruler and click two map points. It measures geometry in true degrees and NM; it does not reveal aircraft positions.
  • Display space: Hide controls keeps the pilot caption and homing visible while enlarging the plot; Show controls restores the edges. Use the full-screen button for more space; Escape leaves full screen. Close a drawer to clear the plot.
  • Small screens: scroll the workspace vertically on short screens; the scope keeps a usable height. Swipe tool rows and panel tabs sideways for more actions. Close the Aircraft controls panel to return to the workspace.
  • Quick aircraft controls: single-click or tap an instructor aircraft to select it and transmit for D/F. Mouse transmission waits half a second to distinguish a double-click. Double left/right mouse click on the same aircraft commands a continuous left/right turn without an extra D/F request. Dragging only pans. Below the scope, Left now / Right now and Stop turn control continuous turns. Enter Heading °T and choose Turn left / Turn right for an assigned heading; this is unavailable with an unserviceable compass. More controls opens Aircraft controls.
  • Clock: Run and Pause affect the shared host. +60 seconds works while paused. Space toggles Run/Pause; T transmits for D/F from the selected aircraft; G steps one minute. A/D select left/right turn, X stops the turn, H requests heading, P requests position and [ / ] changes the selected aircraft. Shortcuts are inactive while typing in a form or using a focused button. Space on a focused button activates that button.

Trails show up to five dots spaced along the flown path, using instructor samples from the last five simulated minutes. Dots appear as enough history accumulates. Reloading clears this display trail; the saved event record remains available. On the instructor desk, homing and the red pilot caption share the control shelf beside the turn and heading controls below the radar. On phones the heading controls are on the left, with homing and the scrollable pilot message on the right. The dark heading field has bright digits and a gold focus border. More controls opens the complete Aircraft panel. Homing and pilot captions remain available with controls hidden. The student instrument layout is unchanged.

3° approach reference

Either desk can open 3° approach for the 10–0 NM table, choose Approach view to frame the final, and show or hide the approach marks. Distances start at the synthetic runway threshold, not the VOR. The table shows altitude in ft MSL and height above aerodrome, rounded to 10 ft.

The geometric reference is altitude MSL = aerodrome elevation + threshold crossing height + distance NM × 6076.12 × tan(3°). It does not command descent or check obstacle clearance. The instructor sets elevation, crossing height, QNH, transition altitude (TA) and transition level (TL) in Edit airspace → Aerodrome & environment. Both desks show QNH/TA/TL; zero TA/TL means unset. Student view controls do not change these shared settings.

03 / BUILD

Bring in the local map

Upload a PNG or JPEG, up to 5 MB, 4096 pixels on either side and 16 megapixels. In This device mode, the image stays in browser storage and is shared with the student tab. In Online room mode, the current uploaded map is also stored in the private exercise room for admitted controllers. Raster display starts off; use Map or the Declutter drawer to show it on each desk.

  1. Set the image’s full width in nautical miles from a known chart distance.
  2. Set the chart origin’s horizontal and vertical location as percentages from the image’s left and top edges.
  3. Rotate the image clockwise to align its north with the scope’s true north. Adjust opacity so routes and symbols remain legible.
  4. Check at least two known points against their displayed bearing and range before using the map.

This is manual image calibration, not automatic georeferencing. East is positive x; north is positive y; the chart origin is the local coordinate origin. A VOR may have a separate east/north offset.

Select a published Indian aerodrome

Open Edit airspace, or Configure airspace during setup, to choose an aerodrome from the dropdown and load its airspace. Seven dated presets cover Hindan, Pune, Jodhpur, Chandigarh, Gwalior, Agra and Bareilly. They use the public India AIP edition effective 3 September 2026. The catalogue includes regional prohibited/restricted/danger areas and published route sections crossing a 250 NM region around the selected base. Full source endpoints are retained, so a crossing leg can extend outside that region. It is a bounded regional selection, not the complete national network. The published LFAs and control zones are different objects: a CTR is not substituted for an unavailable LFA. Published restricted LFAs carry an R boundary and an LFA label.

Loading pauses the exercise and applies the selected chart package. Check its route and boundary coverage, the reference station, existing traffic positions and map alignment before restarting. Both desks see the same chart; only the instructor changes it. Arc boundaries are sampled approximations. See each area’s notes for missing or approximated detail. This catalogue is a dated snapshot, not a live NOTAM feed or an exhaustive list of Indian bases.

Use Route overview to fit the loaded network and Local view to return to the aerodrome. Open Chart briefing for official AAI ENR 6 chart links and their stated edition dates, ENR 3 route references, published leg limits, station details and the shared briefing. The instructor can add runway, transition altitude, ATC frequencies, weather and coordination instructions. The instructor must set the exercise runway and operating conditions; selecting a real base does not imply that the synthetic preset runway is the published runway.

Type routes from AIP India or Jeppesen charts

Set the chart origin first, or load an aerodrome preset. Paste one fix per line as NAME, latitude, longitude. Decimal coordinates and compact DMS such as 172600N, 0782500E are accepted. Alternatively enter east/north nautical miles. Coordinates are projected onto a spherical local chart for synthetic training.

Enter the route designator and saved fix names separated by spaces, commas or arrows. Select India AIP, Jeppesen or Instructor chart as its source; record the chart revision, reference and published levels. Jeppesen chart images are supplied by the instructor; the simulator does not contain a Jeppesen chart database.

Mark prohibited, restricted and danger areas

Choose the category and designator, copy the published lower/upper limits and reference, then enter a polygon’s coordinate pairs or a circle’s centre and radius. Prohibited areas use red solid outlines with faint cross-hatching; restricted areas use salmon dashes; danger areas use red dotted lines. Local-flying outlines use red long dashes and control zones use cyan dash-dot lines. Published LFAs legally classified as restricted remain R areas. Interiors are almost transparent. Labels and the legend identify the category without relying on colour alone. Inactive areas remain outlined. Activation is an instructor exercise setting; the simulator does not infer entry permission or current real-world status.

Fixes and routes

Enter a named fix and its east/north distances, or click empty instructor scope space to fill the coordinate inputs. Saving an existing fix name updates it. Enter route fix names in order, separated by commas.

ATS routes have an open/closed control. Conditional routes also have a simulation-time window and altitude limits. A new clearance onto an unavailable route is rejected. If an assigned route closes or expires in flight, the aircraft continues its current heading with a warning until the route becomes available or the instructor issues a new clearance. It does not freeze. The instructor can edit or close a route during the exercise. Referenced fixes/routes cannot be deleted while in use.

04 / TRAFFIC

Build a roster of 1–24 aircraft

Traffic setup presents every selected aircraft as an editable row on larger screens or a labelled card on phones. Start with 101, then change the count from 1 to 24. Each row has a callsign, free-text aircraft type, initial bearing and range, heading, altitude, speed, turn rate, climb/descent rate, compass-unserviceable setting and spawn delay in seconds.

Bearing places the aircraft relative to the configured reference station; heading determines its direction of travel. They are independent. New rows have distinct default bearings, ranges and headings, with 1000 ft between successive initial altitudes. These are starting values, not an assessment of separation.

Reducing the count hides rows without discarding their edits; increasing it restores them. Configure airspace also preserves your roster draft and shows the current aerodrome. Choose Create session to apply the complete visible roster together. Callsigns must be unique. Cancel leaves the roster unapplied.

The title, runway heading and QNH belong to the same setup. No family selection is needed. Manual QGH exercises use the ordinary aircraft and pilot controls; there is no separate QGH mode selector. Aircraft scheduled to appear later count toward the 24-aircraft limit.

Add traffic or use a starting exercise

In Edit airspace → Exercise & traffic generation, add an individual aircraft or generate a route-based traffic stream. The stream uses the aircraft form's speed and altitude and starts each aircraft at the route's first fix; a spacing interval of zero creates simultaneous traffic.

The three optional presets remain available. Loading one replaces the current attempt:

  • Crossing routes: crossing and longitudinal traffic on ATS routes, with a conditional-route option.
  • Arrivals & holding: converging arrivals, holding and final-approach sequencing.
  • Departure / arrival: an arrival, a departure on the ground and circuit traffic.

Routes, reports, timing and clearances share the same aircraft state. Aerodrome, Approach, Area and QGH procedures use this one simulation.

05 / PSEUDO-PILOT

Fly the controller’s instruction

The student sends a typed controller call. The instructor selects that aircraft, interprets the call and applies the matching structured control. This workflow uses no microphone recognition or voice control.

  • Heading / speed / altitude: issue the selected target. Aircraft turn and change altitude over time.
  • Direct / route: fly to a named fix or follow an authored route.
  • Hold: choose the fix, inbound course, turn direction and leg time. Resume releases the hold. The training racetrack uses a simplified direct entry; it does not model protected entry sectors.
  • Quick turns: select an aircraft, then double left-click for a left turn or double right-click for a right turn. On phones tap the aircraft and use the arrow buttons below the plot. These turns continue until Stop turn. Hover on a laptop to inspect aircraft data. Heading-directed turns: set a heading in the flight controls, then use TURN LEFT or TURN RIGHT. With compass unserviceable these become LEFT NOW / RIGHT NOW for timed turns. STOP TURN and Continue heading immediately level the turn.
  • Orbit: ORBIT LEFT / RIGHT repeats continuously at the selected turn rate. LEAVE ORBIT / HOLD finishes the current orbit lap, then flies straight on its entry heading. A same-side Orbit command cancels a requested exit; Stop turn or Continue heading exits immediately.
  • Conditional instruction: arm execution at a future elapsed time or after passing a specified fix. It does not execute early.
  • Aerodrome: taxi to the holding point, line up, then take off. Taxi, takeoff and landing performance are simplified. Landing requires an aircraft established on a usable final; go-around provides a missed-approach training action.

Altitude and flight level

Choose QNH for a target altitude in feet MSL, or Standard for a flight-level value (for example, 120 means FL120). The host retains MSL geometry and uses a bounded training conversion of 27 ft per hPa. Set the exercise QNH before issuing levels. Climb to requires a target above the aircraft’s actual altitude and Descend to requires a target below it. A conditional instruction is checked again when due. This is not a full atmospheric or temperature model; route altitude limits and scope truth labels use MSL feet.

Pilot readbacks

Accepted clearances create a deterministic pilot caption and aircraft transmission. Enable Local pilot voice to hear an installed English voice. The browser must provide a local voice; no cloud speech service is used. Sound is off initially and is independent of D/F timing.

D/F, heading reports and compass failure

Choose D/F transmission only for a neutral pilot acknowledgement that activates the bearing indication without adding a heading or position report. Choose Report heading or Report position explicitly when that information should be delivered. For instructor-authored speech, choose Custom pilot message and enter the text.

Mark Compass unserviceable in setup or the aircraft conditions controls for compass-failure training. A heading report is then unavailable; neutral D/F and position reports remain separate actions. Use timed turns and Stop turn for manual QGH practice, with the instructor deciding the procedure. No QGH mode selector or microphone is required.

Fix reports and received captions remain in the delivered-report history. A new transmission supersedes the previous channel message. Release transmission starts the configured frozen D/F hold. New setups use 10 seconds; Aircraft controls → Bearing display accepts 2–30 seconds. Older saved scenarios without this setting retain 2 seconds. Changing a setting does not alter a hold already in progress.

06 / STUDENT

Your permitted picture

Enter your name and the instructor’s six-digit PIN, request admission, then choose Ready — open my scope after the instructor admits you. The instructor controls the shared clock.

The central scope has the map, routes, reporting points and a bearing ray during aircraft transmissions. It has no live aircraft targets, aircraft range labels or hidden truth tracks. Static range rings, route labels and approach references are available.

The homing instrument sits in its own side dock below the pilot caption, clear of the drawing area. Move it between the left and right corners with D/F ⇄. Choose QTE for true bearing from the station to the aircraft, or QDM for the magnetic direction toward the station under the exercise variation setting. During a transmission the scope highlights the ray from the configured station. For a VOR, its outbound magnetic radial uses the exercise magnetic reference. Where published declination is unknown, QDM uses a clearly labelled TRAINING REF 0°E assumption. The instructor may change this training value in Aircraft controls → Bearing display. It is not presented as a published station declination. The VOR can be offset from the chart origin. Ground aircraft and aircraft within 0.25 NM of the station have no valid bearing. After transmission release the complete indication, including the sampled magnetic-reference value, freezes for the configured hold duration and then disappears. Aircraft motion continues independently during the hold; no new position is revealed.

Your local controls: drag/scroll to pan, use Ctrl + scroll or +/− to zoom, and use Centre station/Home, Route overview or Local view to reframe. Declutter, the two-point ruler and 3° approach reference are available on the student desk. The instructor’s route and boundary selection appears on both consoles; students can inspect it in Declutter. Pan, zoom, labels, the ruler and approach reference remain local. The instructor controls aircraft, chart content and shared settings.

A pilot report can explicitly deliver a position, range or level. Treat it as a report at its recorded time. It does not turn the student scope into a surveillance display.

Select each aircraft to maintain a manual strip. Enter estimates, clearance records and notes, then Save strip. Incoming reports do not replace your entries. A written strip amendment does not by itself issue a clearance. Send the controller call separately.

07 / PROCEDURAL DECISIONS

Practise the separation method

The exercise supports decisions involving vertical separation, longitudinal time or distance, lateral routing, approach and holding sequences, runway operations and wake turbulence. The instructor selects the applicable criteria and evidence for each aircraft pair.

  1. Record the document/paragraph and local procedure.
  2. State the method’s prerequisites and the minimum applicable to the exercise.
  3. Record what reports, estimates or coordination information the student actually had.
  4. Assess the separation basis: established, procedural-rule deficiency, or insufficient information.
  5. Use the event record to explain when the basis changed and what corrective instruction was issued.

Instructor proximity and common-fix timing cues measure configured training values. They do not automatically establish compliance with every Doc 4444 provision. Lateral, navigation-performance, wake and runway applicability can require instructor judgement beyond geometric spacing. Incorrect but validly entered training decisions are not silently corrected.

Source basis

Reference: ICAO Doc 4444, 16th edition (2016), consolidated through Amendment 13, applicable 27 November 2025. Chapter 5 addresses separation methods; Chapters 6–7 cover approach and aerodrome procedures; Chapter 8 separately addresses surveillance services. Consult the applicable provision and local authorization before setting a training criterion.

08 / RECOVERY & DEBRIEF

Keep the exercise and its evidence

The browser saves accepted actions and exercise progress in local browser storage (IndexedDB). Each instructor room saves its own exercise. Refreshing reconnects to that room while its worker is alive. After a restart, choose Resume this tab’s saved exercise to reopen it paused. Open instructor setup creates a separate room; exporting is the portable backup between devices. Browsers can reconnect without creating a second clock.

Download Scenario + map before replacing an exercise. The file includes its calibrated raster and scenario state. Import restores a validated file paused and clears transient radio activity.

Use End exercise to stop traffic for debrief. It clears radio and prevents Run, Step, clearances and pilot transmissions while the exercise is ended, including after a host restart. Choose Reopen exercise from the scope notice or Review drawer to keep the same aircraft, elapsed time, strips, reports and event history. It reopens paused; select Run when ready. Use Pause for an ordinary break. New traffic setup, a sample preset or an import instead replaces the current exercise. The instructor’s Review drawer shows recent commands, pilot reports and scenario events with simulation times. The saved record is bounded to the latest 500 events, 500 reports and 200 controller calls; it is not a complete replay recorder. Compare those events with the student’s delivered reports and manual strip. Download the attempt to retain it before beginning another.

Back closes a drawer or returns through setup to entry; Return to current exercise reopens your retained session. Leave desk ends this tab’s session.

No pilot audio?

Enable Local pilot voice and check that an English voice is installed in the browser/operating system. Captions and D/F continue without audio. No microphone permission is needed.

No aircraft on the student scope?

This is the intended procedural display. Select a pilot transmission from the instructor desk to obtain the D/F ray and homing reading.

Nothing moves?

Check the shared clock is running. Step advances only while paused. If it says ENDED, choose Reopen exercise, then Run. If Run is waiting for a controller, ask them to press Ready or remove the absent controller in Session. A scheduled aircraft waits until its spawn time. If a route is blocked, read the warning: the aircraft continues on its existing heading until availability returns or the instructor gives a new clearance.

Session PIN expired or admission ended?

Resume this tab’s saved exercise after a host restart and use the current PIN shown in Session. Students request admission again. New PIN / close current admissions also replaces the PIN and clears admissions; the old PIN cannot be reused.

Separate PCs?

This beta runs on one computer. Use two tabs/windows in the same supported browser profile. Independent devices have independent simulations; their PINs are not shared across the internet. A networked classroom requires a separately implemented and tested connection mode.
